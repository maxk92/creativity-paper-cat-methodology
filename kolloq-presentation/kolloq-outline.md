# Outline for Kolloq Presentation
Context: Presentation in internal research colloquium among colleagues, students and a few guests
Aim: Give an update on the study and preliminary results, try to get valuable feedback from esteemed colleagues for data analysis and methodology as well as ideas for the paper's vision.
Target: ~20 slides, ~20-30 minutes presentation
Experience/expertise in the group: a few psychologists, a lot non-psychologists, mix of sports scientists and computer scientists; therefore, need to explain some non-basic psychological topics and creativity background before diving into the main study

## Section 1 - Introduction general creativity
- introduce creativity as an important skill and construct
- standard definition of creativity (Stein, Runco/Jaeger): product of originality and functionality
- mention that in the past, credibility and reputation of creativity have suffered due to inconsistent terminology and lack of taxonomies
- mention the 2019 special issue in PACA on psychology assessment (barbot2019, barbot2019b); show the taxonomy of Batey (batey2012) (with the cube and the three dimensions)
- mention the main procedures for assessing creativity: divergent thinking tasks to assess the creative process, self-report/other-ratings for creative person, consensual assessment technique for rating creative products

## Section 2 - Introduction creativity in sports/football
- a lot of research has been done on assessing the creative process by means of divergent thinking tasks, adapted to the sports scenario (mostly computer-based tests showing videos and asking for solutions to a game situation)
- Kempe and Memmert 2008 are so far the only ones assessing creativity in competition, with CAT
- the dynamics of creative behavior in competition, as a function of game context and in relation to player traits remain elusive

## Section 3 - Automated Assessment of Creativity
- automated assessment of verbal creativity: LSA, Transformer
- our idea: automated assessment of creativity in sports by means of event (and potentially other) data
- to this end, we need labelled data
- the original project idea was to develop methods for automated creativity assessment
- therefore, we set out to make people judge actions w.r.t. their creativity
- we assumed this was purely a labelling task, but quickly realized that inter-rater-agreement and handling the aggregation of multiple ratings into a single number was far more complex than we figured

### the present study
- therefore, the present study turned into a methodological study, investigating how many rater are needed to reach convergence, how to generate a joint creativity value per action from multiple ratings, and which specific assumptions to make in this process

- derive the problems of relative agreement, rater tendencies,
