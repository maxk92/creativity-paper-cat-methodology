## Themes

1. **This paper is explicitly positioned as paper 1 of a 3-paper sequence** (method → application → automated detection), per `CreativityProject.md` and `1___ResearchPlan.md`. The vault strongly suggests keeping this manuscript scoped strictly to CAT methodology (number of judges, expertise, aggregation/JRT), deferring VAEP-correlation and automated-scoring material to siblings.
2. **The number-of-judges question is already partially operationalized** in `CreativityRatingStudy.md` with a concrete sampling/rater-pairing design (benchmark set rated by 5, then pairwise ratings; citing "5-10 judges is adequate" per Kaufman/Plucker et al. 2008 — not yet a formal vault citekey).
3. **Rater expertise is treated as an open, contested question**, not a settled one: the vault notes juxtapose Amabile's claim that expertise need not be uniform against Plucker/Kaufman/Temple/Quian's finding that domain expertise doesn't always increase agreement (with a speculative football analogy about heavy viewership substituting for formal expertise) and flags Castel et al. (2007, "dark side of expertise") and Baer (2015) as further expertise-related citations — but neither of the latter two has been annotated with actual content yet.
4. **Rating-scale design has already been worked out in detail** (`RatingScaleCreativityInSports.md`): unipolar 7-point novelty×appropriateness scale, FOR rater training, mixed-model and Many-Facet Rasch Model analysis options, with an explicit compatibility bridge to Kempe & Memmert's 0–10 scale.
5. **JRT/IRT-for-judges is flagged everywhere as the key open methodological gap** — appears as "==TBD==" in `CreativityRatingStudy.md`'s own analysis plan, and as an unresolved question in `1___ResearchIdeas.md` and `1___ResearchPlan.md`. The `JudgeResponseTheory.md` concept note itself is essentially empty (a placeholder), meaning the author has not yet written up JRT theory in the vault — it exists only as a citation to `[[@myszkowski2019]]` and a to-do marker.
6. **Construct definition draws consistently on novelty/originality × functionality/appropriateness**, both in the sport-specific German definition note (`CreativityDefinition.md`, citing Runco 2012) and in the rating-scale spec — this is a stable, repeated framing across notes.
7. **CAT is treated by the author as a mature but under-specified-for-new-domains method**: Amabile (1982) established it; Cseh & Jeffries (2019) is the repeated critical touchstone motivating the entire "how many raters/how much expertise" research program.
