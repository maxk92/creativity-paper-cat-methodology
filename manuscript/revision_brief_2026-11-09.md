## B. Intended Introduction arc (for outline-architect and manuscript-writer)

1. Creativity matters (society, PISA, sport); creative *products* are assessed by consensus of judges: the CAT.
2. The reliability problem. CAT reliability rests on aggregation: Spearman–Brown logic (Amabile 1982); a large panel can mask low agreement between single judges (Kaufman et al. 2008a). How many judges are enough depends on the reliability target, the object of measurement (persons vs products), rater expertise and training, the scale, and the product domain. Evidence across domains (from the review): 2–5 trained raters for short homogeneous divergent-thinking responses; random panels of 10 novices average alpha ≈ .53–.66 for poems, stories and engineering designs and converge poorly with experts; about 7–15 non-experts for open-ended design products; adding raters stops helping when person-by-task variance dominates; no rater-number study located for sport, music (headcount), mathematics, advertising, programming. Conclusion: no general required number exists; each domain needs its own evidence (Cseh & Jeffries 2019 list it as an open question; do **not** write that they require domain-by-domain determination; van Hooijdonk et al. 2022 argue each assessment procedure needs its own G- and D-study).
3. The verbal bias and the sport case. The evidence base is dominated by verbal material (divergent-thinking tasks, writing, captions) and static products rated at leisure. Football actions are nonverbal behaviour produced under time pressure in a complex, dynamic environment, observed in competition: an ecologically valid product class for which rating-design evidence is missing. Sport creativity research has concentrated on process measures (divergent thinking in game situations); the few product-rating studies average ratings and report ICCs with about three raters by convention. (Vault + web search must supply support for "mostly verbal"; if the evidence is thinner than hoped, phrase cautiously and flag for the author.)
4. Methods ladder. Raw averages and alpha/Spearman–Brown treat judges as interchangeable and conflate panel size with agreement; resampling and G-theory separate variance sources and project reliability to k raters but still treat raters as interchangeable and cannot correct individual raters; many-facet Rasch models (Primi et al. 2019) estimate and correct severity in incomplete designs but assume equal discrimination (and, in the rating-scale form, a shared category structure); judge response theory (Myszkowski & Storme 2019; Myszkowski 2021; later applications found by web search, e.g. Barbot 2023, Pellegrino 2025 if confirmed) estimates severity, thresholds and discrimination per judge, gives conditional reliability and product-specific precision, and captures relative agreement beyond the limits of the other methods. Hence JRT is the main method here. Whether its extra flexibility is needed, compared with MFRM-type models, is itself an empirical question answered by model comparison (RQ3).
5. The present study: large set of CAT ratings of football actions by 24 raters in a sparse design; three research questions. RQ1 is phrased explicitly as reliability as a function of the number of ratings and the number needed for reliable estimates (observed and simulated); RQ2 rater differences; RQ3 (a) tenability of the Rasch-family constraints, (b) consequences of the model choice for scores.
